1. What is Systems Biology?

Traditional biology studies:

One Gene
One Protein
One Process

Systems biology studies:

Entire Biological System

Instead of asking:

What does Gene A do?

It asks:

How do thousands of genes,
proteins and pathways
work together?
Example

Human cell contains:

20,000+ genes

Millions of proteins

Thousands of reactions

Studying one gene alone often gives incomplete information.

Systems biology studies:

Whole network
Central Idea
Genes
↓
Proteins
↓
Interactions
↓
Pathways
↓
Cell Behavior

Everything is connected.

Viva Questions
What is Systems Biology?
Study of biological systems as interconnected networks.
What is a node?
A biological entity such as a gene or protein.
What is an edge?
A connection between nodes.
What is a hub gene?
Highly connected gene in a biological network.
What is a pathway?
Series of connected biological events.
What is KEGG?
Database containing pathways, genes, diseases and biological information.
What is multi-omics?
Integration of genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics.
Why is Systems Biology important?
Because biological processes arise from interactions among many genes and proteins, not isolated components.
